A well-run Google Business Profile in Nice comes down to a few things: the right primary category, an address that follows Google's rules, genuine reviews arriving steadily and up-to-date hours, Carnival included. This guide walks through each setting in the order I handle them for clients, whether that's a shop in Vieux-Nice, a tradesman in Saint-Isidore or a practice in Cimiez. The big picture is in [my three-layer local SEO article](/en/blog/rank-on-google-in-nice-local-seo/); this one goes deep on layer 2.

## How does Google rank profiles in Nice?

Google spells it out in its [help page on local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en): three main factors decide the order of results on Maps and in the Local Pack (the map block showing three businesses).

| Factor | What Google measures | What you control |
|---|---|---|
| Relevance | How well the profile matches the search | Categories, services, description, attributes |
| Distance | How far the business is from the searcher | Almost nothing, beyond an accurate address |
| Prominence | How well known the business is: links, articles, review count and ratings | Reviews, local press, website, citations |

The same page states that you can't pay to improve your local ranking.

Nice is dense. Someone searching "bakery" on Place Garibaldi won't see the same results as someone in Riquier, a fifteen-minute walk away. You can't work on distance, so all the effort goes into relevance and prominence.

## Which primary category should you pick?

The primary category is the setting that weighs most on relevance. Google asks you to complete the sentence "This business IS a…" and to use [as few categories as possible](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en).

- Pick the most specific category that exists. A trattoria in Vieux-Nice is better off as "Italian restaurant" than "Restaurant".
- Add secondary categories only if they describe what you are, not what you sell. A plumber with no shop doesn't take "Bathroom supply store".
- Check the categories of the three profiles ranking above you for your main search. It's the fastest way to confirm you're competing on the right ground.

Changing the primary category can move rankings within days, in either direction. I change it once, after analysis, and then leave it alone.

## Storefront or service area: what are the address rules?

Google separates two cases, and getting this wrong is one of the most common reasons for a suspension.

If customers come to you (shop, restaurant, practice, estate agency on the Port), you show your address. It needs visible signage and staff present during the listed hours.

If you go to your customers (tradesman, home coach, boat cleaning), you're a service-area business, or SAB: address hidden, service area defined. According to [Google's help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en), you can add up to 20 areas (cities, postcodes), never as a radius, ideally within two hours' drive. For a Nice-based tradesman, the city's four postcodes (06000, 06100, 06200, 06300) plus Saint-Laurent-du-Var or Cagnes-sur-Mer usually do the job. A garage in Nice Ouest that takes cars in and also does call-outs is hybrid: address shown and a service area.

### No virtual offices

PO boxes are refused, virtual offices aren't eligible, and a coworking space only counts with clear signage, customers received on site and staff present during listed hours. Registering your company at a business centre on Avenue Jean-Médecin just to "have a city-centre address" puts the profile at risk of suspension.

## Business name: why keyword stuffing backfires

The profile name must be your real name, the one on your sign, invoices and website. Google bans adding a tagline, a service description, hours, location details or a phone number.

"Plomberie Martin" complies, "Plomberie Martin Plumber Nice Emergency 24/7" doesn't. A keyword-stuffed name can survive a few weeks, until a competitor reports the profile and Google renames or suspends it. "Nice" belongs in the name only if it's on your sign.

## Photos, services, products, attributes: fill them in honestly

Google's [photo guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6103862?hl=en) set the frame: JPG or PNG, 10 KB to 5 MB, 720 × 720 px recommended, minimal editing, videos up to 30 seconds. Start with the frontage seen from the street, with a landmark: for a bar on Cours Saleya, that's what helps people find the door. Then add the interior, the team and real work (a carpenter shows his jobs, not stock photos), spread over the year rather than forty photos on day one.

Fill the services section (or menu for a restaurant) with your customers' words: "water heater replacement", "property valuation", "day boat hire". Products are for shops. Attributes (terrace, wheelchair access, Wi-Fi, contactless payment…) depend on the category: tick every one that's true, since they feed Maps filters and AI-generated answers.

## Posts, Q&A and messaging: what still exists in 2026

Posts (updates, offers, events) are still there. They won't lift a profile on their own, but one post a month about something real (a new menu, a finished job in Cimiez) shows the profile is maintained.

The Questions and answers section is being retired. According to [BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/google-business-profile-qa) and other specialists, Google shut down the Q&A API in November 2025 and the public module disappeared from most profiles in early 2026. In its place Google is rolling out "Ask Maps", a Gemini assistant announced on 12 March 2026 that answers questions from the profile's information and reviews. It launched first in the US and India and hasn't been confirmed for France yet. When it arrives, your profile and your reviews will become the answer.

Built-in messaging (the Google Business Messages chat) ended on 31 July 2024. Depending on the region and the profile, you can now add a WhatsApp or SMS link to your contact details. If the option shows up for you, only add it if someone actually replies.

## How do you get Google reviews legally?

Google says it plainly: more reviews and positive ratings help local ranking. The rules are strict, on Google's side and under French law.

### Google's rules

Google Maps' [user-contributed content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en) bans:

- paid reviews, in cash or in kind, and any incentive (discount, gift, prize draw) in exchange for a review or for changing a negative one;
- selective solicitation, meaning only asking happy customers (the classic "Happy? Leave a review. Not happy? Email us");
- reviews from employees, former employees or partners, and reviews posted on a competitor's profile.

What's allowed: asking all your customers for a review, with a link or QR code generated from the profile, without steering the rating.

### The French legal framework

Since 28 May 2022 (ordinance no. 2021-1734, which transposed the EU "Omnibus" directive), article L121-4 of the French Consumer Code lists as misleading commercial practices posting or commissioning fake reviews, and claiming reviews come from real customers without taking steps to check. These offences carry up to two years in prison and a €300,000 fine (article L132-2), which can be raised to 10% of turnover. The DGCCRF, France's consumer protection authority, has already penalised businesses and e-reputation firms over fake reviews.

### The method I recommend

Ask at the right moment (handing over keys, end of a meal, end of a job), send the link the same day to every customer by SMS or email, and put a QR code at the counter. No rating target, one reminder at most. A few reviews a month beat a wave of thirty in one week, which Google may filter out.

## Replying to reviews: the short method

Google advises short, friendly replies, with no promotion and never any private information about the customer. My rule: reply to every negative review within 48 hours, to most positive ones with a specific sentence (not a pasted "Thanks for your review!"), and sign off.

When a negative review feels unfair, stick to facts: your reply will mostly be read by future customers. If the review breaks the policy (fake customer, insults, conflict of interest), report it from the profile.

## Suspension and video verification: what to do?

A suspended profile vanishes from Maps or can no longer be edited, often after a non-compliant address, a keyword-stuffed name or duplicates. The [official procedure](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4569145?hl=en): fix whatever breaks the rules, then appeal through the dedicated tool with supporting documents (Kbis extract or SIRENE record, an electricity or phone bill in the profile's name and address). You get one appeal per decision, and Google replies by email with no guaranteed timeframe.

To verify a profile or clear up doubts, Google often asks for video verification. The rules: one continuous, unedited recording of at least 30 seconds, filmed on a phone inside the app. It shows the location (street sign, number, nearby landmark), the business (permanent signage, or tools and vehicle for an SAB) and proof that you run it (access to the till, workshop or back room). Review takes up to five business days. Plan the route before you hit record: the street corner, the sign, then inside.

## Your profile and AI: what Gemini, AI Mode and ChatGPT do with it

Google rolled out AI Overviews and AI Mode in France from 22 July 2026. For a local query ("a good Italian in Vieux-Nice open on Sunday"), tests published by Whitespark and Human Level show AI Mode listing Google Business Profiles with their rating, hours and a summary drawn from reviews. Google hasn't published which fields are used or how they're weighted. The link with Maps is documented, though: the Gemini API's "Grounding with Google Maps" documentation describes answers built from place information and user reviews.

On the ChatGPT side, OpenAI doesn't publish its local data providers, and industry analyses point to several sources (the Bing index, directories, review sites). Hence the value of identical name, address and phone everywhere, and an up-to-date Bing Places listing.

1. Profile claimed and verified, with two people holding owner or manager access.

4. Secondary categories limited to what you are.

10. Services or menu written in your customers' words.
11. Every true attribute ticked.
12. Real photos (frontage, interior, team, work), added throughout the year.
13. Special hours entered: Carnival from 9 to 28 February 2027, public holidays, closures.
14. Review link sent to every customer, with no incentive or filtering, and a reply to every negative review within 48 hours.
15. One post a month and a quarterly check of the profile, Bing Places and what the AIs say about you.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are Google My Business and Google Business Profile the same thing?

Yes. Google renamed Google My Business to Google Business Profile in late 2021. You now manage the profile directly from Google Search or the Maps app.

### Can you have a profile in Nice without business premises?

Yes, as a service-area business: address hidden and service area defined. A registered-office address or virtual office, however, isn't accepted.

### How long does it take to get into the Local Pack in Nice?

It depends on how competitive your category and neighbourhood are. Fixing a category or address can move the profile within days; prominence (reviews, links, press) builds over several months.
