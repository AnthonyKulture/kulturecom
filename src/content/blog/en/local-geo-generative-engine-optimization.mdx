**Local GEO** is about getting your business named when someone asks ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini or Google for "a good [trade] near me". AI engines don't guess: they cross-check your Google profile, your reviews, directories, your website and what other sites say about you, then recommend the businesses all those sources agree on.

I'm a freelance SEO & GEO consultant based in Nice, and this is the work I do for businesses on the French Riviera. Think of this page as the hub: how the mechanism works, which signals matter, and a simple way to measure where you stand. Each lever has its own in-depth guide, linked below.

## What is local GEO?

**GEO**, short for *Generative Engine Optimization*, covers everything that makes a business visible in answers written by generative AI engines. **Local GEO** is its geographic branch: getting recommended for queries that pair a service with a place ("interior designer Nice", "dentist open Saturday in Antibes", "who can rebuild my website in Cannes").

The difference from local SEO is the shape of the answer:

| | Local SEO | Local GEO |
|---|---|---|
| Where you show up | Local Pack (map + 3 listings), Google results | ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, AI Overviews, AI Mode answers |
| What the searcher sees | A list of links and listings | A handful of names, often with a reason |
| What gets assessed | Relevance, distance, prominence of the listing | How consistently sources describe your business |

They aren't rivals. **Local generative engine optimization** sits on top of local SEO: if an AI engine can't find your business in the sources it reads, it can't recommend it. I've laid out that three-layer setup (search, Maps, AI) in [dominate Google in Nice](/en/blog/rank-on-google-in-nice-local-seo/).

## How does an AI engine pick a provider "near me"?

For a local question, none of these engines answers from memory. They run a search, pull pages and listings, then write from what they found. What differs is **where they look**.

- **Google (AI Overviews and AI Mode)** relies on its index and its usual ranking systems. In its [AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) documentation, Google explicitly names the **Google Business Profile** as a way for local businesses to appear in its AI answers. The full picture since the July 2026 launch in France is in my guide to [AI Overviews and AI Mode for local businesses](/en/blog/google-ai-overviews-ai-mode-local-business/).
- **ChatGPT** searches the web when a question calls for it. OpenAI doesn't publish its local data providers; industry analyses point to Bing's index, directories and review sites.
- **Perplexity** queries its own index and has announced data deals with Yelp and Tripadvisor. I break it down in [how to get cited by Perplexity](/en/blog/get-cited-by-perplexity/).
- **Gemini** draws on Google's ecosystem, Maps included.

The practical upshot: you don't "submit" a business to an AI the way you'd submit a site to a directory. You make it **consistent and easy to find everywhere these engines look**. An engine that reads three different opening hours, two addresses and a truncated name will hesitate, and quote a better-documented competitor instead.

## The 6 signals AI engines read for local queries

### 1. Your Google Business Profile

It's the most direct source for Google, Gemini and AI Mode, which pull its rating, hours and a summary of its reviews. Exact primary category, listed services, special hours, recent photos: it's all in the [Google Business Profile guide for Nice](/en/blog/nice-google-business-profile-guide/).

### 2. Reviews, and what they actually say

The rating matters; the wording matters just as much. A review that says "rebuilt the website of an Old Town restaurant in time for the season" gives an AI engine something to match against a specific request. Ask for detailed reviews, within the rules, and reply to every one.

### 3. Consistent directory citations

In local SEO, a **citation** is a mention of your name, address and phone number (your **NAP**) on a third-party site. AI engines use citations to confirm that a business exists and where it is. The ones that matter most in France: Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, PagesJaunes, plus your industry's directories (Tripadvisor or TheFork for a restaurant, Doctolib for a medical practice). One rule: **the same NAP everywhere**, down to the character.

### 4. LocalBusiness structured data

Schema.org `LocalBusiness` markup describes your business in a machine-readable format: business type, address, hours, service area. Google says it isn't required for its AI features; Bing says it uses it for its models. Its real value is that it forces you to clean up details that must match the page and the profile. There's an annotated JSON-LD example in the [LocalBusiness schema markup guide](/en/blog/localbusiness-schema-markup-guide/).

### 5. Service pages that name the city

An engine looking for "small business website design in Nice" retrieves pages that answer that question, not a generic homepage. Every real service deserves its own page, with the city, the area covered, turnaround and a price range in plain text. One page per service, not one per keyword variant: Google treats mass-produced city pages as scaled content abuse.

### 6. Mentions on third-party sites

This is the most underrated signal in **local SEO and AI**. Generative engines like to quote what others say about you: a local news story, a "best… in Nice" roundup, a partner's website, a trade association, a chamber of commerce event. One editorial mention on a site the engine trusts outweighs ten directory listings.

## How to measure your local GEO in 30 minutes

The simplest test is also the most telling: ask AI engines what your customers ask, and write down what they say.

1. **Write 10 realistic prompts**, phrased like a customer, not an SEO. Mix discovery queries ("best web designer in Nice"), need-based queries ("who can build a website for my restaurant in Antibes?") and brand queries ("what do people say about [your business]?"). If your clients are English-speaking expats or visitors, test in English too.
2. **Run them in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Mode**, in a fresh session: private browsing or a temporary chat, with no history or memory steering the answer.
3. **Log four things** per answer: are you mentioned? In what position? Which sources are linked? Are your details correct?
4. **Run each prompt two or three times.** Answers shift between attempts; what counts is how often you appear, not a single result.

A grid like this is enough (the values are just an example of how to fill it in):

| Prompt | ChatGPT | Perplexity | Gemini | AI Mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Best web designer in Nice | mentioned 2/3 | not mentioned | … | … |
| Agency to build a restaurant website in Antibes | … | … | … | … |

The most useful column is usually **sources cited**. If Perplexity names three competitors based on the same local blog post, that's where you need a mention. The full version, with GA4, Search Console and server logs, is in [how to measure AI visibility](/en/blog/measure-ai-visibility-chatgpt/).

## FAQ

### Does local GEO replace local SEO?

No, it builds on it. AI engines pull their information from search indexes, business profiles and directories. A business that's invisible in local search has very little chance of being recommended by an AI.

### Can you pay to be recommended by ChatGPT?

Not inside the answers. Citations in a ChatGPT answer can't be bought. OpenAI has opened ad placements in France, labelled as such and kept separate from answers; that's a different lever, covered in the [ChatGPT Ads guide](/en/blog/chatgpt-ads-advertising-guide/).

### How long before you see results?

Profile and NAP fixes show up on Google within a few weeks. Third-party mentions and new pages usually take several months before AI engines pick them up consistently. That's why it pays to measure every month with the same prompt panel.
