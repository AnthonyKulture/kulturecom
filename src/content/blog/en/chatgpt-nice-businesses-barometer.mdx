On 6 November 2026, I asked ChatGPT 50 questions that a local or a visitor asks to find a restaurant, a plumber or a notary in Nice, three times each. The 150 answers name 579 different Nice businesses. Yet the list changes from one answer to the next: for a given question, 57% of the businesses recommended appear in only one answer out of three, and just one in four comes back every time. Restaurants, beaches and food shops are the most stable. For a dentist, a hairdresser or an osteopath, being recommended is close to a lottery.

As far as I know, this is the first barometer of its kind in Nice. I will run it every month with the same questions, and the full data can be downloaded at the end of this article.

The 50 questions cover food and drink, tourism and leisure, trades, health and wellbeing, services and shops. They are written the way people talk to an assistant ("I need a plumber in Nice for an urgent leak, who would you recommend?"), with no business name, and were asked in French. I sent them to the OpenAI API with web search on and the location set to Nice. The full method and its limits are set out further down.

## ChatGPT's recommendations change from one answer to the next

Ask ChatGPT the same question twice and you will not get the same list. For each question, I counted how many of the three answers each business appears in:

- 57% of businesses appear once;
- 19% appear twice;
- 24% appear all three times.

For 4 questions out of 50, no business comes back in all three answers: guided tours, air-conditioning installers, website builders and wedding photographers. For "Who can build my company's website in Nice?", the three answers name 23 different businesses.

This is not specific to Nice. In January 2026, SparkToro and Gumshoe ran 12 prompts nearly 3,000 times through ChatGPT, Claude and Google's AI surfaces, and the same list of brands came back [less than once in a hundred runs](https://searchengineland.com/ai-recommendation-lists-rarely-repeat-study-468076). For a business, being cited once by ChatGPT says little. What matters is how often it comes back.

## What does ChatGPT rely on to recommend a place in Nice?

The 150 answers contain 1,527 links to 475 different websites. Two kinds of source dominate.

**The businesses' own websites.** 52% of the websites cited in an answer belong to a business it recommends, and 74% of answers link to the website of at least one business. That is a floor: I only counted websites whose address appears in the answer. ChatGPT reads these pages to describe the business, its specialities, opening hours or prices. For the website-building question, for instance, it quotes the prices shown by the freelancers it cites.

**Local guides and rankings.** The most cited third-party websites are city guides:

The sources change with the sector. For leisure, they are tourist guides (explorenicecotedazur.com, nicetrotter.fr). For trades, directories and comparison sites (allovoisins.com, autour-de-moi.com) plus the tradespeople's own websites. For lawyers, almost only the firms' websites (35 links out of 38). For notaries, by contrast, directories such as notaires.fr or the Guide des notaires, and public websites.

What carries little or no weight:

- **Google**: no answer cites a Google Business Profile or Google Maps as a source. ChatGPT sometimes advises you to "check the reviews", with no link to Google.
- **Social media**: no links to Facebook or Instagram.
- **The press**: Nice-Matin appears in a single answer, Le Monde in 6.
- **Specialist platforms**: TheFork and the Michelin Guide in 2 answers each, Doctolib in none.

## Recommendations that point to websites that no longer exist

On 6 November 2026, I opened the 610 distinct links cited by ChatGPT. 11 lead nowhere: page not found, a domain with no website, or a suspended site. Six of them are the websites of recommended businesses: a yoga studio, a wedding photographer, a wine shop, an air-conditioning installer, a renovation company and a locksmith. One of these businesses is even cited in all three answers to its question, while its website reads "this site is blocked".

A dead website does not mean the business has closed. But nothing in the answers flags it: ChatGPT recommends whatever its sources give it. I did not count as dead the 56 links that refuse automated visits, nor paywalled articles.

## What this means for a business in Nice

These results give four practical leads to a business that wants ChatGPT to recommend it.

1. **A website that says clearly who you are.** It is the source ChatGPT cites most. Your trade, your neighbourhood, your opening hours and your prices should be spelled out on a page crawlers can read. My guide to [appearing in ChatGPT](/en/blog/seo-geo-appear-in-chatgpt/) covers the technical side.
2. **Be listed in local guides.** 10bestnice.com, cotedazurfrance.fr, nicetrotter.fr or the Petit Futé carry more weight than TripAdvisor in these answers. Check whether you are listed, and with the right details.
3. **Track a frequency, not a rank.** A single answer proves nothing. Ask your customers' questions several times, every month, as explained in the guide to [measuring AI visibility](/en/blog/measure-ai-visibility-chatgpt/).
4. **Keep your Google Business Profile up to date.** ChatGPT does not cite it, but it remains your shop window on Google, Maps and Google's AI answers. The [Google Business Profile guide for Nice](/en/blog/nice-google-business-profile-guide/) walks you through it.

For restaurants, the guide to [restaurant SEO in Nice](/en/blog/restaurant-seo-nice/) applies these points to the platforms of that sector.

## Method and limits of the barometer

**The questions.** 50 questions in French, across six sectors, worded the way a consumer asks an assistant, with no business name. The list stays the same from one edition to the next, so that months remain comparable.

**Collection.** On 6 November 2026, each question was asked three times, in three separate conversations, through the OpenAI API: model `gpt-5-search-api`, web search on, approximate location set to Nice, no instructions and no history.

**Extraction.** A second model (`gpt-6-luna`) listed the businesses named in each answer, leaving out categories and platforms. I then merged variants of the same name, such as "Le Chantecler" and "Le Chantecler (Hôtel Negresco)", or "Maître" and "Me" before a lawyer's name: first by automatic comparison, then with a third model (`gpt-6.1-sol`), reviewing every grouping. Doubtful cases, such as two locations of the same chain, were kept apart. The same day, I opened every cited link to check that it works.

**Limits.**

- The API is not the ChatGPT app. The model may differ, and the app takes your account, your history and your exact location into account. The barometer shows a trend, not the exact answer each person will see.
- 50 questions do not cover every trade, and three runs give a frequency, not a precise probability.
- Automatic extraction can miss a name or split one badly, for instance when two shops are cited together.
- Being cited is no proof of quality, and not being cited says nothing about a business.

**Disclosure.** I am an SEO and GEO consultant in Nice: helping businesses get cited by AI is my job. No business paid or was told in advance. Two questions concern my own field. For "Who can build my company's website in Nice?", ChatGPT cites me in one answer out of three; for "Which SEO consultant should I choose in Nice?", in none. None of my clients appear in the answers.

## Frequently asked questions

### How does ChatGPT choose the businesses it recommends in Nice?

In this barometer, ChatGPT relies mainly on two sources: the businesses' own websites, which make up 52% of the websites cited, and local guides such as 10bestnice.com, cotedazurfrance.fr or the Petit Futé. The places listed in several guides are the ones that come back most often.

### Why doesn't ChatGPT always recommend the same places?

Each answer is written afresh, from a web search that can bring up different pages. Across 50 questions asked three times, 57% of the businesses cited for a question appeared in only one answer out of three.

### Do Google reviews matter for being recommended by ChatGPT?

None of the 150 answers cites a Google Business Profile or Google Maps as a source. ChatGPT relies on businesses' websites and local guides instead. Your Google profile remains essential, though, for visibility on Google and in its AI answers.

### How can a business in Nice get recommended by ChatGPT?

Start with a website that says clearly what you do, where and at what price, then make sure you are listed in the local guides ChatGPT cites. After that, measure every month how often you appear for your customers' questions.

### How often is the barometer published?

Every month, with the same 50 questions. The next edition is planned for early December 2026.
