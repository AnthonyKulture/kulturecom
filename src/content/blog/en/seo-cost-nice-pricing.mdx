The cost of SEO in Nice comes down to three things: how competitive your search terms are, the state of your website and how much content needs writing. As a benchmark, an experienced freelance SEO consultant in France charges around €500 a day before VAT, and ongoing work is usually billed monthly, more expensively at an agency than with a freelancer. Here is where those figures come from, what they cover, and how to size the right budget for a business in Nice, Cannes or Monaco.

## What are the market rates for SEO?

There is no official price list. Two public sources still give useful benchmarks, as long as you read them with their limits.

The [rate barometer published by Malt](https://www.malt.fr/t/barometre-tarifs/marketing/consultant-seo), the French freelance platform, puts the 2026 average day rate of an experienced SEO consultant at around €500 before VAT. Early in a career (0 to 2 years of experience), it drops to around €280. The figure only covers freelancers active on Malt, so it is not an average of the whole French market, but it is the benchmark most people follow.

For monthly retainers, the most quoted study comes from [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-pricing/), which surveyed 439 SEO providers worldwide (page updated in August 2024, amounts in US dollars):

| Type of provider | Average monthly retainer (Ahrefs) |
|---|---|
| Freelancer | about $1,349 |
| Agency | $3,209 |
| Consultancy | $3,250 |
| Local SEO (all providers) | $1,557 |

The same study found that 78.2% of providers charge a monthly retainer. These averages blend countries with very different price levels (the US pulls them up, India pulls them down). I use them as an order of magnitude and for the trend, which I also see on the Riviera: for the same scope, an agency costs noticeably more than an independent consultant, because it pays for sales staff, project managers and offices.

## Freelancer or agency in Nice: what changes besides the price?

Mostly, who does the work. With a freelancer, the person you meet is the one who audits your site, fixes the markup and writes the recommendations. At an agency you often talk to a salesperson, then a project manager, and the technical work goes to someone else.

An agency makes sense when the project needs several skills at once: a full rebuild, paid campaigns across several countries, content produced at scale. For a small business, a shop or a professional practice in Nice, one consultant usually covers the whole need, for less.

How you are billed matters as much as the rate:

- by the day: common for technical missions and large accounts; you pay for time rather than a deliverable;
- fixed fee: an audit, a migration, a Google profile to rebuild; the scope is agreed in advance;
- monthly: ongoing work (fixes, content, reporting); check the minimum commitment before you sign.

## What makes a local business's SEO budget go up or down?

Two neighbouring businesses on the Rue de France can have budgets that differ by a factor of three. These are the factors that actually move the bill.

| Factor | Lighter budget | Heavier budget |
|---|---|---|
| Competition on the search term | "cordonnier Libération" (a cobbler in one Nice district) | "avocat divorce Nice", "estate agent Cannes" |
| State of the site | recent, fast, well built | old, slow WordPress, duplicate pages, indexing errors |
| Target area | one district, one town | the whole Riviera, several countries |
| Languages | French only | French, English, Italian (Monaco, Cannes, hospitality) |
| Content to produce | a few service pages to rewrite | a guide per service, area pages, a blog |
| Google profile | already complete, steady reviews | to create, to verify, or duplicated |

Competition comes first. A locksmith in Saint-Roch competes with a handful of local rivals and a few national platforms. An estate agency targeting "villa for sale Cap d'Antibes" competes with luxury networks that have been publishing for years, in several languages. The work is completely different, and so is the price.

International demand weighs heavily here too. In Monaco or Cannes, many customers search in English, and each language needs real pages linked with hreflang tags (the method is in the guide to a [multilingual website on the French Riviera](/en/blog/multilingual-website-french-riviera/)). A translation bolted on at the end of a project costs more than a structure planned from the start.

## Does GEO blow up the bill?

No, and be wary of offers that sell GEO as a separate product with a surcharge. GEO, or Generative Engine Optimization, means getting cited by generative engines such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini or Google's AI Overviews. It rests on the same foundations as SEO.

Google says so in its [documentation on AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features): there are no additional requirements or special optimizations needed to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and you don't need to create AI text files or add special schema.org structured data. On the ChatGPT side, [OpenAI's crawler documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots) explains that OAI-SearchBot is what surfaces websites in ChatGPT search, and that sites which block it in robots.txt are not shown in ChatGPT search answers. Checking that file takes five minutes.

What takes time, and therefore budget, is what also serves SEO: pages that answer your customers' questions clearly, identical business details everywhere (site, Google, directories), reviews, and mentions in local press or guides. The part that is specific to GEO is mostly measurement: regularly asking ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini your customers' questions and noting who comes up. The method is in the article on [measuring your visibility in ChatGPT](/en/blog/measure-ai-visibility-chatgpt/).

ChatGPT Ads is a separate case: it is advertising, so it adds a media budget on top of the service fee. Ads appear below the answers and, according to OpenAI, do not change what the answers say. Details are in the [ChatGPT Ads guide](/en/blog/chatgpt-ads-advertising-guide/).

## What is free, and what you can do yourself

Part of local SEO costs nothing but time. Creating and managing your Google profile is free, as Google confirms on the [Google Business Profile page](https://business.google.com/en-all/business-profile/). Search Console, the tool that shows which searches your site appears for, is free too.

Before paying anyone, you can:

1. complete your Google profile: the right primary category, opening hours, photos, description (the guide to [Google Business Profile in Nice](/en/blog/nice-google-business-profile-guide/) lists 15 points to check);
2. ask every happy customer for a review, and reply to all of them;
3. make sure your name, address and phone number are identical on your site, Google, Pages Jaunes and the platforms of your trade;
4. set up Search Console and look at the queries that already bring you visits.

For a neighbourhood shop, those four steps are sometimes enough. A provider becomes useful when the site itself is holding you back, when competition is strong, or when you simply don't have the time.

## Which promises should make you walk away?

Google has a whole page on this, ["Do you need an SEO?"](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/do-i-need-seo). It states that no one can guarantee a number one ranking on Google, and that if someone promises it, you should find someone else.

The same page warns you to be wary of:

- SEO companies and consultants who contact you out of the blue by email;
- anyone who claims a special relationship with Google or priority indexing;
- anyone who requires a link to their own site from yours (Google says you should never have to do this).

I would add two signals from what I see at Riviera businesses that get in touch with me: a long contract with no written deliverable each month, and a report that only talks about "rankings" for searches nobody types. A good report shows real Search Console queries, phone calls and quote requests.

## What should you ask before signing?

Google recommends interviewing a provider before hiring them. The questions on its list, adapted to a local business:

- Can you show me examples of your work and give me client references?
- Do you follow Google's rules (Search Essentials)?
- What results do you expect, and in what timeframe?
- What experience do you have in my industry, and in my city?
- How will we communicate, and will you tell me about every change made to my site?

Also ask what happens when the contract ends. Content, Search Console and Google Business Profile accounts should stay in your name.

## Frequently asked questions

### What budget should a shop in Nice plan for SEO?

For a neighbourhood shop, part of the work is free: a complete Google profile, reviews, Search Console. A one-off audit is then often enough to know what to fix. Monthly follow-up makes sense when competition is strong or the site needs regular new content.

### What is the day rate of a freelance SEO consultant in France?

Around €500 before VAT for an experienced profile, according to Malt's 2026 rate barometer, and around €280 early in a career. The barometer only covers freelancers active on Malt.

### Do you have to pay extra to appear in ChatGPT or Google's AI Overviews?

No, not for the answers themselves. Google says no special optimization is required for AI Overviews, and ChatGPT search shows sites its OAI-SearchBot crawler can access. Only ChatGPT Ads is paid, because it is advertising shown below the answer.

### Can a provider guarantee the top spot on Google?

No. Google states that no one can guarantee a number one ranking in its results, and advises finding someone else if you are promised one.
