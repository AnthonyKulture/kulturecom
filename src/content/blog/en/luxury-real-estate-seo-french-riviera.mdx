Luxury real estate SEO on the French Riviera rests on three things the property portals won't do for you: neighbourhood pages written by someone who knows the ground, one Google profile per office that actually opens its doors, and a clean multilingual site. That same content is what ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity pick up when a buyer asks them which agency to call about Cap d'Antibes or Monte-Carlo.

## Who searches for a luxury property on the Riviera, and how?

Two audiences, searching in two different ways. The first is the buyer, sometimes from abroad, who types "villa for sale Cap Ferrat sea view" or asks ChatGPT which agencies know Cap d'Antibes. The second is the seller, the person who gives you a listing mandate: they search "estimation villa Californie Cannes" or "agence immobilière Mont Boron", in French, from the Riviera itself.

That second audience matters more than people assume. In the [2025 review of the Alpes-Maritimes housing market](https://www.cote-azur.cci.fr/app/uploads/2026/03/20260317_CP-BILAN-MARCHE-IMMOBILIER-DHABITATvdef.pdf), published in March 2026 by the Nice Côte d'Azur Chamber of Commerce, the Alpes-Maritimes Chamber of Notaries reports that 69% of buyers come from the département and that foreigners account for only one buyer in ten. Those figures cover the whole market, and I couldn't find a public equivalent for the luxury segment alone. They still show that an agency in Nice or Cannes also lives off local owners, who search Google in French.

Monaco is a small market in numbers and a huge one in value. The [IMSEE 2025 Real Estate Observatory](https://imsee.mc/en/our-events/monaco-statistic-releases-the-2025-edition-of-its-real-estate-observatory), published in February 2026, counts 493 sales and resales worth €5.9 billion in total. With so few deals, a handful of precise searches is enough to make the difference.

## Why do property portals outrank your own site?

Because you hand them your best content. A listing copied word for word onto your site and several portals creates that many near-identical pages. Google picks one to show, and in my experience it is often the portal's, which has more authority and more links.

Keep the portals, they bring leads. But stop fighting over the property page and win where they are weak:

- neighbourhood and town pages, written with real knowledge of the area;
- guides for foreign buyers (purchase costs, how a sale works in France or in Monaco, everyday life there);
- pages for sellers: valuation, how you market a property, discreet off-market sales;
- a property description on your own site that is longer than, and different from, the one on the portals.

A portal sorts the Cannes listings by price and floor area. It can't explain the difference between La Californie and Super-Cannes, or why an open east-facing sea view is worth more than another. You can.

## Which neighbourhood pages should you build in Nice, Cannes and Monaco?

One page per area where you hold listings and real expertise. Not one page per coastal town with the same text and a new name: [Google's spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) target exactly that, under "doorway abuse", pages aimed at cities or regions that all look alike. No buyer is convinced by them either.

| Area | Typical searches | What the page should cover |
|---|---|---|
| Nice: Mont Boron, Cap de Nice | "Mont Boron villa sea view", "real estate agency Mont Boron" | property types, access, views, schools and travel time to the centre |
| Nice: Carré d'Or, Cimiez | "Carré d'Or apartment for sale", "Cimiez apartment terrace" | buildings, floors, parking, distance to the sea or the parks |
| Cannes: La Californie, Super-Cannes | "villa Californie Cannes", "Super Cannes villa for sale" | views over the Lérins Islands, slopes, access, gated residences |
| Cannes: Croisette, Croix-des-Gardes | "Croisette apartment sea view", "Croix des Gardes villa" | seafront, renting during events, how quiet the area is |
| Cap d'Antibes, Saint-Jean-Cap-Ferrat | "villa Cap d'Antibes", "Cap Ferrat property for sale" | scarce supply, privacy, sea access, confidential sales |
| Monaco: Monte-Carlo, Larvotto, Fontvieille | "apartment Monte-Carlo sea view", "Larvotto apartment for sale" | differences between districts, residency, steps specific to the Principality |

Put in facts only you can write: the streets with open views, the residences with a good name, the real travel time to the airport. AI tools pick up that level of detail because they can't find it anywhere else.

## How should a luxury agency set up its Google profile?

One profile per physical office, with signage and staff present during the listed hours. The [Google Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) are specific on several points that hit real estate directly:

- a mail-forwarding address or virtual office does not qualify for a profile;
- a coworking desk only qualifies if it has signage and you receive customers there with staff on site;
- real estate agents count as individual practitioners: an agent who deals with the public and can be reached at the verified address gets their own profile, whose title holds only their name;
- a solo agent representing a brand uses the "Brand: First name Last name" format.

An office in Nice, one in Cannes and a desk in Monaco means three profiles, each with its own category, hours, photos and reviews.

## Why does the site need to be multilingual, and how do you do it right?

Because some luxury buyers search in English, Italian or German, and they won't find a page written in French. Google can serve each person the right version, as long as the site tells it how with hreflang tags.

The rules in [Google's documentation on localized versions](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/localized-versions) are short:

1. Each language version lists every other version, and itself.
2. If page A points to page B, page B must point back to A, otherwise the tags are ignored.
3. `x-default` names the page served when no language matches the browser.
4. A translated page is not a duplicate. It becomes one if the main content stays in French.

I give each language its own URL (`/en/`, `/it/`) and only translate the pages someone can keep up to date: one good neighbourhood page in English beats four machine-translated languages. The technical detail is in the article on [multilingual websites on the French Riviera](/en/blog/multilingual-website-french-riviera/), and the Principality's special case in the one on [SEO in Monaco](/en/blog/seo-monaco-international/).

## Photos and structured data: what does Google read?

In luxury property the pictures sell, but Google can't see a sea view in a file called `IMG_0042.jpg`. Its [Google Images documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-images) asks for short, descriptive file names, informative alt text tied to the page content, and images placed near the text that talks about them. For a villa: `villa-cap-antibes-pool-sea-view.jpg`, and alt text that describes what the photo shows rather than a string of keywords.

On the code side, Schema.org has a `RealEstateAgent` type, a subtype of `LocalBusiness`. I put it on each office's page with the name, address, phone, opening hours and official profiles, exactly as they appear on the Google profile. The full example is in the article on [LocalBusiness schema markup](/en/blog/localbusiness-schema-markup-guide/).

## How do AI tools recommend a real estate agency?

ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity build their answer from pages they find online and name the businesses that come up across several sources. According to [OpenAI's help page on ChatGPT search](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-chatgpt-search), ChatGPT can also rely on third-party providers for local information such as business listings. Your Google profile, directory listings and reviews therefore count in those answers too.

Usage is growing fast. BrightLocal's [Local Consumer Review Survey 2026](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/), run with 1,002 US consumers, found that 45% had used ChatGPT or another AI tool to get local recommendations, up from 6% a year earlier. Those are US numbers, but some of your buyers come from New York or London.

These are the kinds of questions I test for an agency, in several languages:

| Question asked to the AI | What it is trying to confirm |
|---|---|
| "Which luxury real estate agency for Cap d'Antibes?" | the agency appearing in sources about Cap d'Antibes |

| "Faire estimer une villa à Super-Cannes" | a seller page, area expertise, reviews from owners |
| "Agenzia immobiliare di lusso a Nizza" | an Italian version, consistent name and address |

To carry weight in those answers you need to exist beyond your own site: local and international press, neighbourhood guides, interviews, agency network rankings, detailed Google reviews. The general method is in the article on [local GEO](/en/blog/local-geo-generative-engine-optimization/).

## Where should you start?

If I took over the SEO of a luxury agency in Nice, Cannes or Monaco, I would work in this order:

1. Check the Google profiles: one per real office, the right category, recent photos, reviews asked from every seller and buyer.
2. Write the three or four neighbourhood pages where the agency holds the most listings, in French, then in English.
3. Set up hreflang, then check the return links in Search Console.
4. Rewrite the property descriptions on the site so they differ from the portal versions.
5. Add `RealEstateAgent` markup and name the photos properly.
6. Test about fifteen questions in ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity every month, and note the sources they cite.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a luxury agency outrank the portals on Google?

Yes, on neighbourhood searches and seller searches, rarely on a single property page. Portals have more authority, but they publish little content written by specialists of an area, and that is where a local agency wins.

### Should every agent have their own Google profile?

Google provides for it for each agent who deals with the public and can be reached at the verified address during the listed hours. The profile title holds only their name. For an agency with three or four well-known negotiators it is useful; for a large team, the office profile is often enough.

### Which languages should a Riviera agency's site use?

English first, then the languages of your actual buyers, which you know from your listings and incoming enquiries. Each language gets its own URL and reciprocal hreflang tags.

### How do I know whether ChatGPT recommends my agency?

Ask it your clients' questions, in several languages and a few days apart, then note which agencies come up and which sources are shown. Those sources tell you where to look for mentions.
